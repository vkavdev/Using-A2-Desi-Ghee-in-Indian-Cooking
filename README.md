**What the A2 Label Tells You**

**How Ghee Fits Into Everyday Indian Cooking**
Ghee brings a rich, rounded smell that shifts how a dish feels, even when there's barely any of it in there. A lot of cooks put it in right at the end, stir a little into rice, or finish dal with it. In a Tamil Nadu kitchen it sits comfortably with pepper, cumin, curry leaves, lentils and rice, without pushing everything else off the plate. Begin with a modest amount and keep tasting, because how much is right depends on the dish and on you. Ghee is an ingredient. Treat it as part of the meal rather than a shortcut to a health outcome.

**Use Ghee for Tadka and Sauteing**
Making a quick tadka, warm a little ghee in the pan and then put in whatever spices or aromatics the recipe asks for. Hold the heat steady so the spices open up without catching. Curry leaves spit when they meet hot fat, so put them in with care and keep your hands and face back from the pan. Then pour that seasoning over dal, over vegetables, or over whatever else you've got ready. Ghee will do for sautéing too, when its flavor belongs in the recipe. For anything that needs to taste neutral, a different cooking fat is the better choice.

**Bring Its Aroma to Rice, Roti, and Sweets**
One small spoonful is enough to finish hot rice, take the dryness off a roti, or put a familiar smell through a sweet dish. With payasam and other desserts, stick to the order the recipe gives so the fat works into everything rather than splitting away. On rice or flatbread, put the ghee in just before it goes to the table and adjust from there. The flavor is distinct, which means it comes through more in something plain than in a dish with heavy seasoning. Use less than you'd guess at first, and only add more if the balance asks for it.

**Choose and Store Ghee With Care**
Read what's on the label, and go for a jar that states its contents plainly and tells you how to store it. Shut the lid between uses, and always reach in with a clean, dry spoon so nothing wet or leftover gets into the jar. Keep it where the label says, out of the heat and away from direct sun. When you open it, pay attention to how it smells and looks, and leave it alone if something isn't right. Used carefully, a smaller jar stretches a long way in everyday cooking. The right one is whichever matches your household on flavor, on sourcing information and on the instructions it comes with.
